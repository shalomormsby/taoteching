## 保 (Bǎo) — To Keep Safe

*Say "bao," dipping. Four lines across four chapters, and it is the only one of the holding words that is not about your grip.*

**Locked: 保 → "keep safe."** *(Never "preserve," never "safekeeping," never "maintain.")*

### The graph: a person carrying a child

> 保，**養**也。从人，从𤓽省。𤓽，古文孚。
> *"保 is **to rear**. From 人 (rén — a person) and an abbreviated 𤓽; 𤓽 is the archaic form of 孚 (fú)."* — 說文解字

孚 is a hand over a child — the graph behind *brooding*, *hatching*, and, by a short road, *trust*. In the bronze forms 保 is drawn plainly: an adult, and an infant carried against the back.

So while 持 (*chí*) grips, 執 (*zhí*) takes into custody and 守 (*shǒu*) keeps a post, **保 is the one that carries.** Its definition is not a word for holding at all. It is 養, *to nourish* — the same character 河上公 (*Héshàng Gōng*) uses of the sage at chapter 5, 聖人**愛養**萬民, *"the sage loves and rears the people."* You keep a child safe by feeding it, not by guarding a wall.

### The character runs in both directions, and that is the whole of it

At chapters 15 and 67 a person keeps something safe:

> **保**此道者不欲盈 (15) — *"those who keep the Tao safe like this have no wish to be full."*
> 我有三寶，持而**保**之 (67) — *"I have three treasures. I hold them and keep them safe."*

At chapter 62 the Tao keeps a person safe:

> 善人之寶，不善人之所**保** — *"the treasure of those who are masterful, and what keeps safe those who are not."*

Both commentators make the Tao the agent there, not the object. 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*) gives the outcome in four characters — **保以全也**, *"保, because it keeps them whole"* — where 全 (*quán*) is the character locked to *whole* in `quan-全.md`. 河上公 is warmer and more specific:

> 道者，不善人之**保倚**也，遭患逢急，猶自知悔卑下。
> *"The Tao is what the unmasterful lean on for safety. Meeting trouble and sudden danger, they still know to repent and humble themselves."*

保倚 — *to be kept safe by, to lean on.* He uses the same compound at chapter 67 for the three treasures: 我有三寳，抱持而**保倚**. One English across all four lets the two directions answer each other. **At chapter 15 you keep the Tao safe; at chapter 62 the Tao keeps you safe.** That reciprocity is in the character and nowhere in the syntax, and it disappears the moment the four lines take four words.

### The chime, and what it costs

保 (*bǎo*) and 寶 (*bǎo* — treasure) are homophones, and the book prints them together twice — 善人之**寶**，不善人之所**保** (62) and 我有三**寶**，持而**保**之 (67). 說文 on 寶 is 珍也, *"a precious thing,"* built from 宀 a roof over 玉 jade and 貝 a cowrie shell: a hoard indoors. 王弼 separates the pair cleanly at 62 — **寶以為用也** / **保以全也**, *"treasure, because they put it to use; keeping safe, because it keeps them whole."*

English cannot chime here, and the entry records the loss rather than manufacturing a fix. What matters is that the two are not the same word: 寶 is what a thing *is*, 保 is what you *do*.

### What is set aside

**"preserve"** is a conservator's word — jars, specimens, a condition maintained against decay. 保 is 養, growth, and the object of the verb at chapter 67 is three living dispositions, not three artifacts.

**"safekeeping"** turns a verb into a facility. At chapter 62 it also makes the Tao a passive receptacle where both commentators make it the thing that acts.

**"maintain"** is bureaucratic and belongs to the register the whole edition is built to keep out. It is also 守's territory, not 保's — a maintained thing is a kept post.

**"safeguard"** and **"hold on to"** both put the hand back. There is no hand in this graph.

### What no rule can enforce

***"guard"* is not on the forbidden list, and cannot be.** 守 stands with 保 in chapters 9 and 67, and *guard* is 守's word in both. A rule keyed to 保's presence would fire on 守's own line. The two characters sit two lines apart at chapter 9 — 不可長**保** and 莫之能**守** — and nothing in this repository can tell one from the other there. See `shou-守.md`, `chi-持.md`, `zhi-執.md`.

### Left open

**Chapter 62's 所保 carries a recorded fork.** The Dunhuang witness reads 所不保, with a negation that reverses the clause: the Tao becomes what the unmasterful are *not* kept safe by. The silks use a single graph in both the 寶 and 保 positions, so they cannot arbitrate. We keep the base text; the fork is in `sources/variants.yaml`.
