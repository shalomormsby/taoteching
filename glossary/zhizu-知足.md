## 知足 (Zhī zú) — Knowing You Have Enough

*Say "jr dzoo." A lock-note: one recurring phrase, one settled rendering, so it stops drifting.*

**The characters.** 知 (zhī) = to know, to recognize. 足 (zú) is a picture of a **foot** — the lower leg and foot — and its first meaning is exactly that: *foot, leg*. The very same character also, from very early, means **"enough, sufficient, ample."** We won't pretend the bridge between the two senses is settled, but the resonance is worth keeping: your foot is *what you stand on* — your footing, your ground. To have 足 is to have ground enough to stand. So 知足 — "know-enough" — is, almost literally, **knowing the ground under you is already enough to stand on.** Contentment as sound footing, not as resignation.

**The lock.** Render 足 as **"enough"** — plain, concrete, unstuffy — and 知足 as **"knowing you have enough."** Where a line needs a *noun for the inner state*, use **"contentment."** That is the whole rule: the plain verb is *enough*, the state-noun is *contentment*, and nothing else. Retire **"sufficiency"** (stiff), **"fulfilled"** (drifts — it slipped into Ch 33), and **"satisfaction"** (too psychological). Two faces of one plain word.

**How it reads, locked, across the book:**

- **Ch 33** — 知足者富 → *"Those who know they have enough are rich."* (contentment as the only durable wealth)
- **Ch 44** — 知足不辱 → *"Know when you have enough, and you are never disgraced."*
- **Ch 46** — 禍莫大於不知足 → *"no greater disaster than not knowing when you have enough"*; and the triple-足 close, 知足之足常足矣 → *"the **contentment** of knowing you have **enough** is **contentment** forever"* — the plain verb and the state-noun working together to echo the original's three hammered 足.

**Don't confuse it with its sibling.** 知足 travels beside 知止 (*knowing when to stop*, Ch 32 & 44). They rhyme but they are not the same: **知足 is about *having*** — being at peace with what you already hold; **知止 is about *doing*** — halting the action before it overreaches. *Enough*, and *stop*. Keep them distinct in English, too.
