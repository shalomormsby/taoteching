# Chapter 15

## Translation

The ancient masters  
Were subtle-fine and dark-penetrating —  
Too deep to be fathomed.  
Because they cannot be known, I can only force a description:

Careful, like crossing a frozen river.  
Hesitant, as if wary of neighbors on four sides.  
Dignified, like a guest.  
Yielding, like melting ice.  
Sturdy, like uncarved wood.  
Expansive, like a valley.  
Mixed, like muddy water.

Who can be like muddy water, then sit and gradually become clear?  
Who can be still, then move in ways that gradually stir life?

One who holds the Tao has no desire to be full.  
Because they don’t seek to become full,  
They can become weathered, and have no interest in appearing new.

## Notes

**猶兮若畏四鄰 → "Hesitant, as if wary of neighbors on four sides."** *(2026-08-31, Pass C, `WORKLIST.md` T2-11.)* *(was: "Watchful, aware of danger from all sides.")* **The danger was invented.** There is no 殆 (*dài* — danger) in this chapter and no threat in the Chinese, so no check could ever have seen it; it surfaced only because `--english "danger"` had to be run before locking that word elsewhere.

**河上公 (*Héshàng Gōng*) supplies what the line is actually about:** 其進退猶猶如拘制，若人犯法**畏四鄰知之**也 — *"their advancing and retreating is hesitant as if held back, like someone who has broken the law **fearing the four neighbours will find out**."* **Not peril from every side — the fear of being seen.** 鄰 (*lín*) is a neighbour, and 四鄰 is the ordinary phrase for the households around you.

**畏 (*wèi*) flexes here, and it is worth marking.** It reads **fear** in six other lines (17, 20, 53, 72, 74 ×2), always fear *of a thing* — authority, death, what people fear. Here 河上公's gloss is 畏四鄰**知之**, fear *that they will find out*: social exposure, not a threat. *"Fearing the neighbors"* would name them as the danger; *"wary of"* keeps the watching, which is the chapter's whole register. 畏 has no glossary entry yet, and this is the line that will decide its shape.
