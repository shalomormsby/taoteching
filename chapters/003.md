# Chapter 3

## Translation

By not exalting the exceptional, the people have no cause to contend.  
By not prizing rare goods, the people do not become thieves.  
By not displaying the desirable, the people are not perturbed.

Therefore the sage  
Empties hearts and fills bellies  
Weakens striving and strengthens the bones of the people.

Without creating desire or cunning,  
Without causing the cunning to act,  
Without effort, the sage leaves nothing out of order.

## Notes

**難得之貨 → "rare goods," and the line was circular before.** *(2026-08-30, Pass C, `WORKLIST.md` T2-14.)* *(was: "By not valuing the precious, no one becomes a thief.")* 不貴難得之貨，使民不為盜 (*bù guì nán dé zhī huò, shǐ mín bù wéi dào*) is a **causal claim about what a ruler does** — the goods are hard to get, the ruler prizes them, people steal. *"Do not value the precious"* puts the value in the object, so it argues in a circle and **the ruler drops out of the sentence**, which is the one place the sentence points. 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*) keeps the cause where the Chinese has it: 貴貨過用，貪者競趣，穿窬探篋，沒命而盜 — *"prize goods beyond their usefulness, and the greedy race for them: they tunnel through walls, rifle chests, and steal at the cost of their lives."*

**貨 (*huò* — goods) was wearing 貴's word, in a line where 貴 is the verb.** *Precious* belongs to 貴 (*guì*) at ch 70, 72 and 75; here 不貴 is *does not prize*. 貨 is now locked to *goods* book-wide — see `glossary/huo-貨.md`, which reads the three cowrie characters apart: 貨 is 貝 (*bèi* — a cowrie shell) under 化 (*huà* — a body turning over), **a shell that changes hands**; 寶 (*bǎo* — treasure) is a hoard under a roof; 貴 is a position on a price scale.

***"Rare" and not "scarce," and the reason is this chapter's third line.*** *Scarce* means **a need going unmet**. 河上公 (*Héshàng Gōng*) names the goods — 好珎寶，黃金棄於山，珠玉捐於淵, *"fine treasures; gold left in the mountain, pearls and jade abandoned in the deep"* — and **nobody needs jade**. If the wanting were a real shortfall, honestly felt, the ruler could not be blamed for it. But line three says where the wanting comes from: 不見可欲，使民心不亂 — *"do not display what can be desired, and the people's hearts are not disturbed."* **Display, then appetite, then theft.** 說文 agrees at the level of the graph: 盜 (*dào* — thief) is 私利物也。从㳄 — *"to take private profit from a thing; it follows 㳄 (*xián* — drool)"* — **a person salivating over a dish.** The chapter blames the first link.

**The triad was lopsided in the Chinese and our English had flattened it.** The three objects are 賢 (*xián* — the talented, one character), 難得之貨 (four), 可欲 (*kě yù* — what can be desired, two): **the middle line is deliberately the heaviest.** *"The exceptional / the precious / the desirable"* imposed a neat symmetry by shrinking the longest object to two words.

**民 restored to the middle clause.** All three clauses are 使民X — 使民不爭 / 使民不為盜 / 使民心不亂 — and ours had *"the people"* in the first and third and *"no one"* in the second. This uses no word the stanza does not already carry, and **settles nothing about the deferred 民/人 call** (`WORKLIST` T4-1). 不貴 also reads *prizing* now, matching ch 20's 貴食母 and ch 64.
