# Chapter 19

## Translation

Cut off sagehood, abandon cleverness, and the people benefit a hundredfold.  
Cut off humaneness, abandon duty, and the people return to devotion and tenderness.  
Cut off cunning, abandon profit, and there are no thieves.

These three, taken as cultural form, are not enough.  
So give them something to belong to:  
see the undyed silk, embrace the uncarved wood,  
diminish the self, diminish desire.

## Notes

*Revised 2026-08-28 under the 仁 and 慈 locks, with the Guodian fork recorded at the same time. Fork in `sources/variants.yaml`; reasoning in `notes/manuscript.md` · Ch 19. This closes `PLAN.md` → G1.*

**絕 → cut off, 棄 → abandon, and they are two different violences.** 絕 is 斷絲也 — *Shuowen*: *"to break silk thread,"* from 糸 (*mì* — silk), 刀 (*dāo* — knife), 卩. A blade on a thread. 棄 is a child over a basket held in two hands, the element glossed 逆子 (*nì zǐ* — an inverted child): **casting out a newborn.** The verse had spent one English word, *abandon*, on both, and had merged the third couplet's two nouns into one phrase.

*Cut off* rather than *sever*, because 絕 must also serve ch 20's 絕學無憂: English *sever* wants a physical connection as its object and balks at abstractions, while *cut off* takes them and keeps the blade. Ch 20 is swept to match. *Abandon* for 棄 is already the book's word at ch 27 (無棄人) and ch 62.

**And the second couplet turns on that.** 棄 casts out the child; the line's payoff is 民復孝慈 — the people **return to devotion and tenderness**, which is the parent-child bond itself. Throw the child away and the childhood comes back.

**These are goods, not evils, and 王弼 says so plainly.** 聖智，才之善也；仁義，人之善也；巧利，用之善也 — *"sagehood and knowing are the best of talent; humaneness and duty are the best of persons; cunning and profit are the best of use."* Three matched pairs of **goods**, held to be insufficient as 文 (*wén* — cultural form, writing), not three vices. Hence *"These three, taken as cultural form, are not enough"* — the earlier *"insufficient decorations for society"* had invented **edicts** and lost 文.

**故令有所屬 → "so give them something to belong to."** 屬 (*shǔ*) is attachment, belonging. 王弼: 故令人有所屬，**屬之於素樸寡欲** — *"attach them to the undyed, the uncarved, and few desires."* The chapter does not end on a prohibition; it ends by handing over something to hold.

**抱 → embrace**, correcting a lock breach: 抱 is *embrace* and 守 is *hold fast to*, and the two are never swapped. **百倍 → a hundredfold**, restored; it had been flattened to *greatly*.

**The oldest witness does not make this attack.** At Guodian (~300 BCE) the passage carries neither 聖 nor 仁 nor 義; the pairs cut off there are different ones, and only 絕巧棄利 is shared. We keep the received text, as at ch 25, and record the fork.
