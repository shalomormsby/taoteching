# Chapter 12

## Translation

The full spectrum of colors makes the eyes blind.  
The full spectrum of tones makes the ears deaf.  
The full spectrum of flavors makes the palate dull.

Galloping in pursuit of prey makes the heart wild.  
Rare goods make actions corrupt.

Therefore the sage acts for the belly, not for the eye.  
They let go of what is out there and take what is here.

## Notes

**故去彼取此 → "They let go of what is out there and take what is here."** *(2026-08-30, Pass C, `WORKLIST.md` T2-13.)* *(was: "Rejects the hungry gaze, chooses gut wisdom.")* 故去彼取此 (*gù qù bǐ qǔ cǐ* — "therefore let go of that and take this") is a **stock closing tag** — five characters, the last line of ch 12, ch 38 and ch 72, and **彼 (*bǐ* — that, yonder) occurs nowhere else in the book.** All three now carry the same English, ch 72's, where the axis was first argued.

**The old line translated the referents and deleted the deixis.** 彼 and 此 (*cǐ* — this) are **spatial** words — *yonder* and *here* — and the chapter's own pair is 目 (*mù* — eye) against 腹 (*fù* — belly): the organ that reaches outward and can never be filled, against the organ that is already here and can be satisfied. 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*) states it exactly: 為腹者，以物養己；為目者，以物役己 — *"one who acts for the belly uses things to nourish themselves; one who acts for the eye uses things to enslave themselves."* 河上公 (*Héshàng Gōng*): 去彼目之妄視，取此腹之養性 — *"let go of that, the eye's reckless looking; take this, the belly's nourishing of one's own nature."*

***"Gut wisdom" was forbidden by this chapter's own locked entry.*** `glossary/xin-心.md` carries a paragraph headed *"Why 'belly' and not 'gut'"* which names *trust your gut* as the import that must not happen — and **cites ch 12 as its evidence.** The chapter used the word anyway, one line below its own correctly rendered *belly*. Two things were wrong with it: it invents 智 (*zhì* — knowing, wisdom), a character not in this chapter, and it swaps the chapter's actual contrast — **inner need against outer craving** — for a modern one, *intuition against analysis*. It is the same failure as *"here, now"* at ch 21: see `notes/translation.md` → *Register — no modern spiritual idiom*.

**The checker could not see it, and now can.** 腹 sits inside 心 (*xīn* — heart) as a `covers:` character, and **`covers:` records a `render:` but never a `forbidden:` list** — so *gut* was unforbidden book-wide. `"gut"` is now on 心's `forbidden:` list, which fires here because 心 is present in this chapter.

***What "the hungry gaze" was worth, and why it still goes.*** It was the most vivid phrase in the chapter. But 目 is already *"the eye"* in the line directly above, so the image is not lost — only not repeated — and *hungry* was borrowed from 河上公's gloss 妄視, a commentator's word rather than the text's.

**難得之貨 → "rare goods."** *(2026-08-30, Pass C, `WORKLIST.md` T2-14.)* *(was: "Rare treasures make actions corrupt.")* **"Treasures" is 寶 (*bǎo*)'s word** — ch 62, 67 and 69 — and 寶 never appears in this chapter. 說文 reads the two apart: 寶 is 珍也, a *precious thing*, its graph 宀 (*mián* — a roof) over 王 (jade) and 貝 (*bèi* — a cowrie shell), **a hoard that does not move**; 貨 (*huò*) is 財也, *property*, 貝 under 化 (*huà* — a body turning over), **a shell that changes hands**. Locked to *goods* book-wide: `glossary/huo-貨.md`.

**This chapter is why "scarce" was refused for 難得.** *Scarce* means a need going unmet — and the chapter's whole hinge is 為腹不為目 (*wèi fù bù wèi mù*), 腹 (*fù* — the belly), the organ that **can** be satisfied, against 目 (*mù* — the eye), the one that cannot. **Scarcity is a fact about bellies. This chapter is about eyes.**

**Still owed here, not fixed:** 妨 (*fáng* — to hinder, obstruct) reads *"corrupt"*, a moral word for an obstruction word. 王弼 (*Wáng Bì*): 難得之貨，塞人正路 — *"goods hard to come by block a person's straight road."* 河上公 (*Héshàng Gōng*): 妨，傷也 — *"妨 means to harm."* That is naturalistic-razor territory and its own item.
